2026-11-02

Acquisitions below 10% are not marked
2026-11-01

Only deals of at least 10% of prior total assets are marked. A serial acquirer making many smaller deals reads as organic growth; the run only stops when unexplained acquisition spend in a single year reaches 10%.

The headline marks acquisitions so readers know when growth is bought; many small deals add up to the same thing.

Companies: all; none checked for cumulative small deals.

Due: before the full S&P 500 run.

Same-business (organic) growth is not measured
2026-11-01

The ten-year headline promises the same accounting basis, not the same business. Organic growth would come from each deal's ASC 805 pro forma disclosure (revenue and earnings as if acquired at the start of the prior year), read and approved company by company.

Reported growth after a large deal mixes bought and grown revenue.

Companies: MSFT, HD, JNJ, PLD (the companies with significant deals): pro formas not read.

Due: when a reader needs organic growth, or when I1 starts.

Significance uses the price test only, not the asset test
2026-11-02

SEC Regulation S-X Rule 1-02(w) has three tests (investment, asset, income); the pages apply only the investment test (price paid against prior total assets). A deal bought cheaply but carrying a large balance sheet is not marked. JPM 2008: Bear Stearns $1,496M paid but $288,731M of assets acquired (18.5% of JPM's $1,562,147M at 31 Dec 2007); Washington Mutual $1,941M paid, $263,649M of assets (16.9%). Both significant under the asset test, neither under the price test, so JPM's 2008 shows no break. The asset test needs total assets acquired, which only 9 of the 58 tagged deals on the 11 pages carry in XBRL; the rest need the 10-K's purchase-price allocation read by hand (about 14 deals at 1% or more of assets).

A distressed purchase is a regime break the price test cannot see; the time-window rule forbids an undisclosed one.

Companies: JPM 2008 found (Bear Stearns, Washington Mutual); others not checked.

Due: before JPM's page is called finished, or before the full S&P 500 run. Parked by James 2026-11-02 ("too much work for now").

closed 2026-11-02, the asset test added alongside the price test (decisions.md, "Significance: price or assets"); JPM 2008 now marks both deals

Companies that left the index are read nowhere
2026-11-02

The readers run on today's list. Of the 693 companies in the S&P 500 at some point from January 2016, 194 left before December 2025 and have no page, no census verdict and no ten-year read. Weighted by the SEC's frames, those leavers were 15.4% of the index's revenue in 2016 (8.8% of pre-tax income), 7.7% in 2020 and 3.5% in 2025. Every sample so far is survivors only; the early years are where the survivorship bias is largest and where the cost measure's fall years sit.

A cost measure read on survivors overstates how well the index held its margins, because leaving the index is correlated with not holding them.

Companies: the 194 leavers; the membership file holds their identifiers.

Due: before the cost measure's sample is called representative; the run itself is mechanical (the readers over 194 more identifiers), the choice to show them on the Chomper page is James's.
