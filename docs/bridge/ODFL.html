Why Old Dominion's operating income fell $183.0M in 2025

Operating income went from $1,544.0M in 2024 to $1,361.0M in 2025 (-11.8%). Shipping less freight cost $527.5M; higher prices made back $206.1M; costs fell, adding $135.5M. Open any row for management's explanation, quoted from the 10-K and linked to the page it came from.

Volume (LTL tons)-$527.5M

Lower freight volume reduced operating income by $527.5 million. Total LTL tons fell 9.1%; the 8.8% drop management cites is tonnage per day, which is smaller because 2025 had one fewer work day (253 versus 254). Management attributes the decline to fewer shipments per day and lighter shipments, against a backdrop of continued softness in the domestic economy.

What the numbers show

  • Management puts a dollar figure on 3 of the 9 cost lines. Those three account for $98.2M of the $135.5M net cost reduction (72.5%). The other $37.3M is not explained in the MD&A.
  • The largest unexplained line is insurance and claims. It fell $17.9M, from $92.4M to $74.4M (-19.4%), with no discussion.
  • Labor cost per ton of freight rose 7.9%. Salaries, wages and benefits fell 2.0% while LTL tons fell 9.1%, so labor cost per ton went from $298.81 to $322.29. Headcount came down, but more slowly than freight. (All salaries, wages and benefits divided by LTL tons; this is an analyst's calculation, not a figure management reports.)
