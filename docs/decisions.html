2026-11-02 7 decisions
A line tagged only in parts is shown summed

When a line on the income statement has no tagged total in a year, and the filing tags it in parts on an axis the statement itself shows (Boeing's cost of goods and services sold, by product and by service), the line is the sum of those parts, as one row under the line's own label. The footer names the parts by the filing's labels. If the parts sit on more than one such axis the build stops; if a line is summed in some years and a single total in others, the build stops ("Member slice"), since the parts may be only a slice of the total. Footing to pre-tax income stays the check, with no new tolerance.

39 of the 55 Footing stops were one cost line tagged only by product and service; reading only totals lost the whole line (Boeing's "$70B gap" is its 2023 cost of goods and services sold, $70,070M). One summed row keeps every label a real filing label and matches how revenue, split the same way on the face, is already shown as one total; per-part rows would have carried revenue labels on cost rows (Boeing's parts are labeled "Sales of products" and "Sales of services"). Of the 55, 36 now pass, 10 stop as Member slice; the 11 built pages are unchanged (James, 2026-11-02).

One line under two tags is one row

When a line carries the same label in the filing but a different XBRL tag in the two 10-Ks, it is shown as one row, only when every tag carries figures, the years never overlap, and the line's sign and section (above or below operating income) agree. The row keeps the latest tag; history.py records every tag with its years in `retagged`, and the page footer names each one ("tagged InterestExpense for 2021 to 2022, then InterestExpenseNonoperating for 2023 to 2025"). The year both 10-Ks show is compared across the tag change, as for any line. No matching on similar labels and no company-specific rules; anything short of the test stays two rows.

Keyed by tag, the line became two rows each jumping to or from nothing: Carnival's interest expense showed -$2.07B and +$1.61B where the real change was -$457M. The census found the pattern on 158 of 322 passing companies; the rule merges every split row on 135 of them (164 rows; 97 are InterestExpense to InterestExpenseNonoperating). 23 rows on 23 companies stay split, 20 where the line changes section or sign between the filings (James, 2026-11-02).

No ratio or growth on a loss base

Nothing on a company page divides or scales by a base at or below zero. The ten-year table leaves the percent change blank when the prior year is zero or negative (a fall from a positive year into a loss still shows, e.g. Carnival Nov 2020 -435.1%). The five-year dollar grid, in a column that starts from a loss year, leaves "if every cost moved in step with revenue" and "beyond revenue" blank and shows each line's whole change instead; a note under the grid names those columns. Together with "No cost-gap bar after a loss year", this is one rule. James will look through the loss cases for a tighter rule later.

Scaling a loss by revenue gave Carnival -$51.20B "in step" against a +$3.44B change in Nov 2022, and percent changes like +3188.7% across a sign flip; the chart's income line already breaks at losses (James, 2026-11-02: "If you get a loss on the base here, I think we need to do something").

Footing allows the filing's own rounding

A year foots when the reported lines miss pre-tax income (or operating income) by no more than half a rounding unit for each figure summed, the subtotal included, with each unit read from the fact's own XBRL decimals. Any gap allowed this way becomes its own row, "Rounding in the filing" (a second row, "within operating income", when the operating subtotal is the one off), so the five-year grid and every later check still add exactly; history.py also records each gap with both values in footing_gaps, and the page footer names each one (year, subtotal, lines against reported, in millions). Rounding rows are not costs, so they do not scale with revenue: their whole change counts as beyond revenue, and pre-tax income's in-step change is taken from the figures that foot. A gap beyond the allowance still stops the build.

Carnival's lines miss by $1M in years where every figure is rounded to the million; an exact rule stopped a real company over the filing's own rounding, while a flat dollar or percent tolerance would be arbitrary across company sizes. Boeing's $70B gap stays a hard stop (James, 2026-11-02).

No marker where the standard does not reach the business

A standard gets no marker on the ten-year chart when the company's own 10-K says it falls outside the standard's scope and no adoption note follows (data/adoptions.json, out_of_scope, with that statement quoted). PGR's FY2016 10-K says insurance contracts are outside ASC 606; its FY2018 adoption section lists no revenue standard; PGR shows no 2018 marker.

Every marker cites the company's own words. A "required this year" marker on PGR asserted a break the filing never claims, while the FY2016 sentence says why there is none (James, 2026-11-02).

Rounding is not a disagreement

When two filings give the same figure at different precision, and the coarser one equals the finer one rounded to its unit (PGR: $62,109M against $62,108.5M), the difference is not listed as a disagreement; the footer says the filings agree apart from rounding. Any other difference is listed with both values.

Listing rounding as disagreement printed pairs like "$62.11B vs $62.11B", which reads as an error; recording real disagreements with both values still holds.

Standards marked in the year adopted

ASC 606 and ASC 842 are marked on the ten-year chart in the year each company adopted them, as read from its own 10-K into data/adoptions.json (year, whether earlier years were recast, accession, verbatim quote). A standard adopted with earlier years recast gets no marker, since the series has no break there. Where no adoption note was found, the marker stays on the year the standard became required; where the 10-K does not state the method, the key says only "adopted".

The required year was wrong for early adopters (MSFT, UNH), and a fully retrospective adoption (AAPL, JPM and MSFT 606, MSFT 842) is not a break; the company's own adoption note is the only source that says which.

Ten-year breaks

An acquisition is marked when its price is at least 10% of the buyer's total assets at the prior year end (SEC Regulation S-X Rule 1-02(w), the investment test), read from each 10-K's own XBRL. ASC 606 and 842 are marked in the year they became required. Every restatement is shown with the figure as first reported.

Marking breaks must be correct, not a judgment call: the SEC's own significance test replaces a threshold of ours, and each deal's size comes from the filing.

Time windows

A window must contain at least one revenue contraction and no undisclosed regime break. Long: 10-year headline only. Medium: 5-year line-by-line detail. Short: 1-year bridge. Acquisitions and accounting changes are marked, never used to shorten a window.

Settles the recurring 10-year question: give each time frame its own window instead of forcing all three into one table.

Anchor on pre-tax income

Every company's lines must add up to pre-tax income; operating income is kept as a subtotal where the company reports one.

Banks, insurers and some industrials report no operating income; pre-tax income is reported by every industry.

Ratios lead

Costs as a share of revenue lead each company page; the dollar grid sits underneath as the drill-down.

Percent of revenue is scale-free and the trend reads straight down a row.

Core measure: flexed budget

Each line's yearly change is split into what it would have done moving in step with revenue, and the remainder beyond revenue.

Shows which costs move when demand changes without hand-classifying costs as fixed or variable.

No cost-gap bar after a loss year

The ten-year chart draws no cost-gap bar for a year whose prior year had zero or negative pre-tax income (prior year as shown in the 10-K that reports both). The Loss strip above already marks those years.

The bar is the pre-tax income at last year's margin minus what was earned; after a loss, last year's margin is negative and scaling it by revenue is not a cost gap. Carnival's Nov 2022 bar read $54.6B "revenue ran ahead of costs" from a -499% margin on 6.4x revenue, against an actual pre-tax change of +$3.44B (James, 2026-11-02).

Loss years break the income line

The ten-year chart stays on a log scale for every company. A year with zero or negative pre-tax income breaks the pre-tax income line and sits on a strip labeled Loss under the plot, with its amount; the line runs only through consecutive profitable years. The axis is set from the profitable years. A year with zero or negative revenue stops the build.

A log scale cannot show zero or a negative, and growth is not defined across a change of sign, so the break marks exactly where slope stops meaning growth. Chosen over a symlog axis (slopes near zero would not mean growth), a linear axis for loss companies only (two chart grammars and the first-year problem back), and a margin axis (repeats the table). Checked on test data, Caterpillar's revenue with invented losses in five patterns.

Deal names from the buyer's 10-K

The chart key names each significant acquisition as the buyer's own 10-K prose names the acquired company, with a trailing legal suffix dropped (HD Supply, SRS, Liberty Property Trust, Duke Realty), read by hand into data/deal_names.json with a verbatim quote and the accession. A significant deal with no record stops the build.

The XBRL member label is not the company's name: HD tags HD Supply as "HD Holdings Inc", and PLD's labels came out lowercase and doubled ("Liberty property trust and liberty property limited partnership").

Recast, not restated

A figure that a later 10-K changed is called recast ("Note 1 - Recast Figures", "as later recast"), never restated.

In accounting a restatement corrects an error (ASC 250); most of these changes are new standards, presentation changes or discontinued operations, and calling them restatements claims an error the company never made.

Fiscal year labels

A fiscal year is labeled from its end date read a week early: a year ending in December shows as the year (2020), any other as month and year (Sep 2020, Jan 2025). The week keeps 52/53-week years on one month: JNJ's year ended 2021-01-03 is 2020, and HD's years all read Jan. Labels name the month a year ends in, not the company's own fiscal-year name (James, 2026-11-02): HD's "Jan 2025" is the year HD calls fiscal 2024.

Labeled by the raw end date, JNJ's years jumped between "Jan 2021" and "2020" and HD's between Jan and Feb, so fiscal years looked missing or doubled.

Company names: one source, one rule

Every company name on the site comes from the S&P 500 list (data/sp500.json) through one function, display_name: a trailing legal suffix is dropped ("Apple Inc." becomes Apple), except a bare "Company" that would leave one word ("Southern Company" stays); words that are part of the name stay (Group, Holdings, Trust, Bancorp); "Home Depot (The)" becomes The Home Depot. Company pages and the Chomper list use the same function.

SEC filer names mix capitals and suffixes ("HOME DEPOT, INC.", "PROGRESSIVE CORP/OH/"), and the list's own names are not uniform; one rule over one source can be checked against a single cell per company.

Ten years at the top, charted in log dollars

Each company page opens with ten years of revenue, operating income (where the company reports it), pre-tax income and pre-tax margin, then a chart of revenue and pre-tax income in dollars on a log scale. Each year with a significant acquisition or a newly required accounting standard gets a numbered marker on the chart, explained in a numbered key under it. Under the lines, a band of bars shows each year's cost gap: pre-tax income at the prior year's pre-tax margin minus actual pre-tax income, both years from the same 10-K; red when costs grew faster than revenue, green when revenue grew faster. Figures a later 10-K changed are listed at the bottom as Note 1 - Recast Figures, and a starred change in the table reads "See Note 1". The chart and the five-year grids carry no explanatory notes. The five-year revenue and income table stays removed.

A chart in multiples of the first year depends on that year (CAT's small 2016 pre-tax income made it read 83x); a log scale keeps one axis, and equal slopes still mean equal growth, without a base year. The bars put a dollar size on costs outrunning revenue. The numbered markers were chosen from four trial displays (markers, shaded bands, labels, swim lanes).
